Accession WP-001 · retail carton

WordPress 1.0

“Miles” — released January 3, 2004

Named for Miles Davis, the first of the jazz codenames. It brought search-engine-friendly permalinks, a post preview pane, and a real plugin API. The number was a promise: no longer a patched b2 — a platform.

The museum manufactured the carton it never had, to period retail conventions: one spectral square, one quiet wordmark, a great deal of air.

The film is original. You will be the first to break it. There is nothing to preserve — copies were always the point.

— the documentation team, all two of us

ii
Chapter one

Before you begin

You will need four things, and patience is not one of them:

  1. A web host — a rented sliver of somebody’s server, about $5 a month in 2004 money.
  2. PHP 4.1 or newer and MySQL 3.23 or newer. Ask your host; they will know.
  3. An FTP client to carry your files up the wire.
  4. The WordPress archive itself — 1.5 MB, smaller than three photographs of your cat.
You do not need to know PHP. You do not need permission. This is the part people found hardest to believe.
1
Chapter one, continued

Carrying it up: FTP

fig. 1 — the whole platform, dragged from the left pane to the right

Set the transfer mode to binary, drag the folder across, and go make tea. On a 2004 connection this took exactly one cup.

2
Chapter two

The famous five-minute install

Other systems of the era asked for compilers, cron jobs, or a phone call to a sales team. WordPress asked for five minutes, and said so out loud:

  1. Create a MySQL database. One will do.
  2. Open wp-config.php in any text editor and fill in four lines: database, user, password, host.
  3. Upload, then visit /install.php in your browser.
  4. Answer two questions: what is the blog called, and what is your email.
  5. There is no step five. That was the boast, and it held.
The phrase “famous 5-minute install” went on to live in the official docs for two decades — one of the great confidence tricks that happened to be true.
3
Chapter two, continued

Running install.php

fig. 2 — the installer, drawn from memory, which is how everyone ran it
Write down the password it generates. WordPress will invent one for you — a little string like a8f3k2 — and flash it once. Generations learned this the hard way. Consider the sticky note, a fine 2004 technology.
4
Chapter three

Your first post

fig. 3 — quicktags: bold, italics, and the two that mattered

Click Publish. Now the strange part: it is already live. No rebuild, no export, no waiting. Refresh your site and there you are, in public, slightly out of breath.

5
Chapter four

Troubleshooting

“Error establishing a database connection.” The most famous sentence WordPress ever wrote. Check the four lines in wp-config.php; one of them is lying to you.

A blank white page. PHP is upset but too polite to say why. Re-upload; check the version; breathe.

It looks wrong in my browser. In 2004 this sentence carried no information. Everything looked wrong in somebody’s browser.

When stuck, ask at the support forums. The software is free; so, it turns out, is the help. This economy of strangers fixing strangers’ blogs at midnight is the actual product.
6
Appendix

Where to go from here

wordpress.org/support — the forums. Bring your error message and your manners.

The Codex — a wiki growing faster than the software it describes.

Your own blog — the best documentation is the one you write after fixing it, posted so the next stranger finds it at midnight.

A manual for free software is never finished; it is only ever forked.

the ancestor

A fork is a family promise.

b2/cafelog went quiet in 2003. Matt Mullenweg wrote that he wanted to fork it; Mike Little answered. WordPress began not by erasing its ancestor, but by carrying its GPL code forward.

The disk is invented. The path it charts is not: one abandoned project, two contributors, and permission to continue.

The plugin that came in the box

Hello Dolly has shipped with WordPress since the 1.2 era. When active, it places a random line from the song in the administration header.

A tiny demonstration that code could hook into WordPress and change what appeared there.

Why keep it?

Because examples acquire biographies. It became joke, ritual, annoyance, keepsake, and a pocket-size explanation of the plugin system.

Specifications

One PHP file. No configuration. No batteries. Activate, look up, smile or deactivate.

Packaging and cartridge are fictional. The plugin, its longevity, and the debate around it are thoroughly real.
